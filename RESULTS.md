# Benchmark and validation notes

Personal log of what I actually measured, on what, and how to reproduce it.
Every number here is copied from a real run on this machine; the raw output
lives in `results/*.json` and is regenerated by `make bench`.

Machine: Apple silicon (arm64), macOS, 18 logical cores. Apple clang 21,
`-std=c++17 -O2 -Wall -Wextra -Werror -pthread`. Python 3.9.6, numpy 2.0.2,
pytest 8.4.2. Pipeline topology for all C++ runs: 6 producer threads, 1
consumer thread, bounded queue capacity 65,536.

against a 1e-9 requirement. Zero violations. Monotone assay profile: yes.

## C++ throughput (results/rt_bench.json)

Reproduce: `make bench` (or `./build/rt bench --out results`)

Unpaced, fault injection off, blocking backpressure, zero loss required and
verified (emitted == accepted == processed, dropped == 0):

| channels | frames | wall s | frames/s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 48,000,000 | 0.813 | 59,019,435 |
| 5,000 | 60,000,000 | 0.656 | 91,450,368 |
| 10,000 | 60,000,000 | 0.595 | 100,880,017 |

Throughput rises with channel count because each producer pushes one batch
per tick per shard; more channels means bigger batches and fewer lock
acquisitions per frame. At 1,000 channels the batch is only ~167 frames.

## Latency at the rated operating point (results/rt_bench.json)

Paced at 100 Hz per channel (a generic rate I chose; each tick is nominally
10 ms of sim time). Ingest to detection latency is wall clock: stamped at
frame emit, measured when the consumer dequeues the batch, sampled on every
8th frame. Zero loss verified at every point.

| channels | target f/s | sustained f/s | p50 | p99 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | 100,000 | 99,957 | 18.4 us | 82.0 us |
| 5,000 | 500,000 | 499,762 | 47.1 us | 156.2 us |
| 10,000 | 1,000,000 | 999,593 | 88.4 us | 267.7 us |

Saturated (unpaced) latencies are queue depth dominated and much worse, p99
around 1.1 ms at 10k channels; that is in the JSON too for honesty.

## Detection quality (results/detection_eval.json)

Reproduce: `./build/rt eval --out results`

Config: 64 channels, 20,000 ticks, seed 20260819, 12 episodes per fault type
(48 total), 1,279,359 frames, zero loss.

| fault type | episodes | tp | fp | fn | precision | recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| overspeed ramp | 12 | 12 | 0 | 0 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| vibration spike | 12 | 10 | 0 | 2 | 1.00 | 0.833 |
| thermal drift | 12 | 12 | 0 | 0 | 1.00 | 1.00 |
| sensor dropout | 12 | 12 | 0 | 0 | 1.00 | 1.00 |

46 of 48 episodes detected. The two misses are vibration spikes whose
injected amplitude landed below the 2.0 alarm threshold (the generator draws
amplitudes from 0.5 to 3.5 on purpose, so some episodes are genuinely
undetectable at this threshold). I am leaving that as is instead of tuning
the threshold to the eval.

Episode detection latency (fault start to first matching alarm): p50 9
ticks, p99 14 ticks (90 ms / 140 ms at the nominal tick).

Detection to trip latency (entering ALARM to TRIP): 58 samples, p50 10.0
ticks, p99 11.3 ticks (100 ms / 113 ms nominal). The floor is set by the 10
tick persistence hysteresis I configured; multi channel coincidences trip
faster. Note 58 trips > 46 detected episodes: after the auto reset cooldown,
a still active alarm re trips the cascade, so long episodes trip more than
once. Configured behavior, but worth knowing when reading the trip count.

## Things I did not measure

* Energy or memory footprints: never profiled, so no numbers here.
* Behavior beyond 10,000 channels or with more than one consumer: the single
  consumer is the eventual bottleneck and I did not push to find the knee.
* Anything on non Apple silicon hardware.
